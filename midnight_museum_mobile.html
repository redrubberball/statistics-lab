04 · Make your accusation

FINAL DEDUCTION
The Moonstone case opened at 8:14 PM. The mysterious person carrying a large case passed the hallway camera at 8:13 PM, not 8:18 PM. Compare the evidence with the interviews.
🟡 Quinn · Exhibit curator

“I was preparing labels in the north gallery from 8:05 until 8:20. My yellow cart stayed beside me.” The inventory scanner confirms Quinn scanned an exhibit label at 8:12 and 8:16 in the north gallery. Those scans do not establish where Quinn was at 8:14.

🔵 Alex · Photographer

“The black case with the silver star sticker in that video is mine. I rolled it through at 8:18. I couldn't have been near the gallery before the theft.” Alex's photo studio is across the hall from the Moonstone display.

🟢 Sam · Technician

“I worked inside the sound booth from 8:10 until 8:20.” An uninterrupted booth camera confirms Sam stayed inside the booth that entire time.

🔎 One more thing from the display

The empty display case contains a small square of blue adhesive tape. The gallery inventory identifies that exact tape as the type supplied to the museum photography team for sealing their equipment cases. That makes Alex worth investigating, but it is not proof by itself.

Which suspect's account is directly contradicted by the evidence?

🕵️‍♀️🔓
CASE CLOSED

The time didn't lie.
The camera did.

Alex's timeline is false. Alex says the equipment case passed the camera at 8:18, after the display opened. But the camera is five minutes fast, so that footage actually records 8:13—one minute before the case was opened.

The blue tape connects the scene to photography equipment, and Alex had a large equipment case nearby. Together, those clues make Alex the strongest person to investigate. Important detective rule: a contradictory statement and suggestive evidence aren't the same thing as proving guilt. Your team should question Alex and check the case before concluding who took the Moonstone.
