01 · accumulation in real life

How far did you go?

A cyclist speeds up steadily. Move the time slider to see how the area under the speed curve becomes distance traveled.

distance = ∫ speed · dt
SpeedAccumulated distance
4.0 min
Speed right now8.0 m/min
Total distance16.0 m

Speed tells you meters per minute. The width of a tiny strip is measured in minutes.

m/min × min = m

Adding all the tiny strips gives total distance. In this example, speed = 2t, so the distance from 0 to T is T² meters.

The cyclist's speed is v(t) = 2t meters per minute.

∫₀⁴ 2t dt = [t²]₀⁴ = 16 m

As you move the slider, the upper limit changes, so the total distance changes too.

02 · fundamental theorem of calculus

Area becomes a function.

Drag the endpoint. The accumulated area is a new function, and its slope at any position is the height of the original curve.

A(x) = ∫₀ˣ 2t dt = x²
f(t) = 2tArea A(x)
2.0
Accumulated area A(x)4.00
Slope A′(x)4.00

When A(x) = ∫ₐˣ f(t)dt and f is continuous, then A′(x) = f(x).

Think of filling a bucket: the amount already collected changes at the current filling rate.

A(x) = ∫₀ˣ 2t dt = x²A′(x) = 2x = f(x)

At x = 2, A′(2) = 4.

The derivative of accumulated area gives the height of the original function.

03 · signed area and net change

What if the rate goes negative?

Water flows into a tank, then drains out. Above the axis adds water. Below the axis removes it. The integral tracks net change, not total water moved.

Δwater = ∫ flow rate dt
Water addedWater removed
6.0 min
Flow rate right now−1.0 L/min
Net change in water+15.0 L

An integral adds signed area: above the axis counts positive and below the axis counts negative.

That makes it useful for tracking changes in position, money, or tank volume. To find the total amount moved, integrate the absolute value of the rate instead.

Our example uses a flow rate of r(t) = 5 − t liters per minute.

∫₀⁶ (5 − t) dt = 12 L

At t = 5 minutes, flow changes from filling to draining. The signed accumulated change is still positive for a while afterward.
