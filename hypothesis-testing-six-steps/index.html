How homework conclusions may be worded

Make the decision about H₀ first; then explain the evidence in the language of the original claim.

Claim locationDecisionConclusion wording
Claim in HₐReject H₀There is sufficient evidence to support the claim.
Claim in HₐDo not reject H₀There is not sufficient evidence to support the claim.
Claim in H₀Reject H₀There is sufficient evidence to reject the claim.
Claim in H₀Do not reject H₀There is not sufficient evidence to reject the claim.

“Fail to reject” and “do not reject” mean the same thing. Neither means the null claim has been proved.

Writing H₀: equality or inequality?

Write the original claim exactly before locating it. A claim such as “at least 80%” is p ≥ 0.80 and belongs on the null side. For the introductory z calculation, we use the boundary value H₀: p = 0.80 and Hₐ: p < 0.80. You may also see H₀ written as p ≥ 0.80 when identifying the full claim.

The equality is always the value used in SE₀ and the reference distribution.

Expected counts, independence, and what if a condition fails?
  • Use np₀ ≥ 10 and n(1−p₀) ≥ 10, not counts based on p̂, for the one-proportion z-test.
  • For simple random sampling without replacement, check n ≤ 0.10N to help justify independence.
  • Example of failure: with n = 30 and p₀ = 0.10, np₀ = 3, so the usual Normal approximation may be unreliable.
  • A z-test is not automatically appropriate just because a calculator produces a number; explain unmet conditions and consider a different method.
P-value shading vs. rejection-region shading

P-value: the area beyond the observed z in the direction specified by Hₐ (both equally extreme tails for Hₐ: p ≠ p₀).

Critical value: the z cutoff determined by α and the test direction; the tail area of the rejection region is α.

The P-value depends on the observed z and test direction; the critical value depends on α and test direction. They always give the same reject/do-not-reject decision when applied consistently.

Technology, tiny P-values, and limitations
  • A displayed P-value of 0.0000 often means a very small probability rounded to four places, not probability exactly zero.
  • A P-value is not the probability that H₀ is true.
  • A statistically significant result is not necessarily a practically important result.
  • The graph is the sampling distribution of the test statistic under H₀, not a graph of individual visitor responses.
